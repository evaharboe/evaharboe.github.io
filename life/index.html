Off the screen.

Yoga classes, races and retreats: the things I spend my time on away from a screen, and the people I get to do them with.

Background

I was born in China and grew up in Stavanger, on Norway's west coast, as part of a Norwegian-French family. Weekends and holidays meant the family cabin: skiing in winter and hiking the rest of the year. Being outside has been part of my life for as long as I can remember.

Yoga started as a fix for handball, because I needed to be more flexible. Before long the yoga had stuck and the handball hadn't.

Running came after I moved to England. In Norway the mountains were always right there, but Surrey only has hills, and they barely count. Running became my way of getting outside. The long runs turned out to be the best part: go slowly, keep your heart rate down, and you actually get to enjoy where you are. The longer the run, the more of it there is to enjoy, which is how getting outside turned into training for a marathon.

I love travelling too. I've been to 35 countries, many of them solo, and the best part has always been the people: meeting new people all over the world and the memories you make together. That's a big part of why I want to run retreats, to bring people somewhere beautiful and give them that same feeling.

Yoga

I did my 200-hour yoga teacher training at House of Om in Ubud, Bali, a mix of vinyasa and hatha with some ashtanga and meditation. Now I teach weekly classes for the Surrey Yoga Society, where I was president for two terms. Classes are open to complete beginners, and the focus has always been on making them feel like a community. Attendance doubled in the year I started teaching.

My classes are usually rocket or power vinyasa, and sometimes slower stretching sessions with more of a yin feel. Lately I've been leaning towards yoga for runners: mobility, strength and recovery for people who spend a lot of time on their feet.

Retreats

Retreats are where it all comes together. I've planned and led three for the yoga society, in England and Cyprus, and these are the kinds I'd love to run next. The active ones keep the yoga to a morning and an evening session, so the day is free for the activity.

  1. Run & yogaMorning flow, a run in the middle of the day, and a slow stretch in the evening. One workshop on mobility for runners.
  2. Hike & yogaMorning flow, a long day in the mountains, and an evening session to recover.
  3. Surf & yogaMorning flow before the waves, and a slower evening practice for tired shoulders.
  4. Ski & yogaMorning flow, a day on the mountain, and a long evening stretch for tired legs.
  5. Pure yogaThree sessions a day, with workshops like handstands and inversions, and plenty of time to rest in between.
